# Teaching quality release — 19 September 2026

The aim is for a child to connect a situation, a model and a calculation, then use that relationship in another question. Coverage and completion counts do not establish this outcome.

## Implemented

All 265 Learn lessons now pause for an explanation grounded in their actual worked question. The child can speak, point, draw or type before revealing a comparison explanation. Reflection is explicitly unmarked; the app does not claim to understand the child's speech or writing. Optional written explanations are not stored or submitted.

Ten reference lessons have specific explanation prompts and transfer questions. Seven of these also replace the earlier sequence with a coherent prediction, reachable model activity, annotated explanation, worked example with pupil turns, and three practice questions that introduce small changes. The other three retain their previously authored investigations and now add the explanation/transfer pause.

Exercises retain seeded mixed checks and recovery support. New guided questions include value-specific misconception feedback and an earlier building-block question for recovery. Unfinished answers, wrong attempts and help are saved immediately on this device; navigation/reloads no longer erase that evidence. Three stars now require every check question correct on the first attempt without optional help. Two stars require at least 60% eventually correct. Prior earned stars remain in history. Weak checks/reviews schedule the next review for the following day. A weak challenge offers the teaching sequence rather than simply another challenge.

Mobile changes include number-bond annotations showing the part taken away, larger movement controls, an algebra balance whose pans grow with the counters, and ratio bars that fit narrow screens while preserving equal unit widths. Question changes bring an off-screen question back into view. Adult guides use readable model names and are available without a teacher account.

## Continuing authoring standard

1. State one observable outcome in language a child can understand. Distinguish the skill practised here from the full syllabus objective.
2. Check a necessary earlier skill before introducing the new one. A child who struggles gets a relevant picture and an explanation.
3. Introduce one concrete question. Keep its quantities, colours, units and object meanings consistent until the explanation is complete.
4. Ask a prediction that gives the following action a purpose. The interaction must expose the mathematical relationship, with undo or reset where appropriate.
5. Explain what changed, what stayed the same, and why. Every annotation must identify something visible. A caption must not silently reveal an answer being assessed.
6. Announce a new example before changing values. Let the child contribute a step before revealing its answer; reduce support across practice.
7. Make each assessment form require a different action. In a visual question the picture carries necessary information; it is not counted as optional help.
8. Explain likely wrong answers specifically. Offer one smaller question at a time, return to the original question, and record help honestly.
9. End by reconnecting the result with the relationship, then suggest a short delayed review.
10. Verify correctness, interaction, keyboard operation and narrow layouts. These checks cannot substitute for observing whether a child understands.


## What still requires human evidence

The expanded catalogue is not all at the depth of these ten reference lessons. The automatic comparison explanation for the other lessons follows their existing worked sequence; it does not replace an expert review of that sequence. A child can still guess a multiple-choice answer. A first-try check is evidence about that attempt, not a mastery certificate.

For an initial pilot, observe a few children at the relevant grade using one reference lesson each. Before helping, record where they stop, what they think the picture represents, and their first proposed step. Ask them to explain the key relationship in their own words. Then give a new question with a changed representation or context. On the next day, try a fresh question before showing an example.

Use a small rubric per task: **independent explanation and solution**, **correct after a specific prompt**, or **still needs teaching**. Record the particular misconception or support, not just stars. Prioritise revisions where the child cannot explain why a step follows, where they copy a visual without understanding it, or where the next-day question fails. These observations are required before claiming that the app reliably teaches unfamiliar material independently.

## Remaining content priorities

- Extend triangle examples beyond the current matching right triangles to oblique triangles and internal/external perpendicular heights.
- Deepen the remaining topic sequences with teacher-reviewed counterexamples and less predictable transfer tasks. Their new explanation pause does not by itself establish that the underlying teaching is clear.
- Use patterns of misconceptions to select the next teaching task; current feedback is question-specific and the review interval reacts to the latest result, but there is no validated diagnostic model of a child.
- Reduce the initial Learn download. Splitting a single large bundle requires a separate loading/performance check on slower mobile connections.

## Basis

The implementation follows the EEF guidance on purposeful representations, worked examples, pupils explaining their reasoning, assessment-informed support and gradual independence. These principles are a design basis, not an effectiveness evaluation of this app. [EEF mathematics guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/maths-ks-2-3).

The existing objective mapping remains tied to the [MOE Primary Mathematics syllabus, updated October 2025](https://www.moe.gov.sg/api/media/92bff26d-b2b4-4535-b868-b8415c744b91/2021-Primary-Mathematics-Syllabus-P1-to-P6-Updated-October-2025.pdf). This release changes teaching and assessment behavior; it does not add or claim new syllabus coverage.
