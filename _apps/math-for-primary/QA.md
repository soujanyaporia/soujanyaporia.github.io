A duplication and consistency audit of all 265 lessons, and the repairs it prompted. Counts below come from the same measurement script run before and after the work.

**Warm-ups now test the lesson's own prerequisite.** Every lesson opened with one of ten fixed questions: 262 warm-ups drew on 30 distinct prompts (88.5% repeated), and "A full ten frame holds one ten" opened 49 lessons. Worse, the question rarely matched the grade: the P6 circle lesson began with "How many little squares cover this rectangle?" and the P3 durations lesson with "Eight counters are here. Three are crossed out." The syllabus map already records each objective's prerequisite, so `build/warmUp.ts` now generates the warm-up from that earlier objective's own topic, picking the lightest of six candidates so the warm-up stays gentler than the lesson. The P6 circle lesson now opens with a P5 composite-area question, and the P3 durations lesson with "Convert 1 min 11 s to seconds". Repetition fell to 18.9%, and every one of the 265 answers verifies. Syllabus order is not difficulty order, though: within a year a mental-method objective follows the written method it simplifies, so the P3 two-digit mental lesson was briefly opening with 613 − 21. A guard now keeps the general starting point whenever a same-topic arithmetic warm-up would be more than twice the size of the lesson's own numbers. Of the 252 generated lessons, 227 open with a warm-up built from their prerequisite; 15 have no mapped prerequisite (the first objective of a topic) and 10 are held back by the guard, and those 25 keep the general starting point. The 13 hand-written lessons and the 10 reference lessons keep their own authored warm-ups.

**Each objective has its own expected misconception.** 284 misconceptions drew on 48 distinct texts (83.1% repeated), and they were attached by topic rather than objective, so a fraction-addition warning appeared on 37 lessons including ones not about adding fractions. All of them were labelled "A tempting shortcut". `depth/catalogue/pitfalls.ts` now names and corrects the error each objective actually provokes, with a worked instance: "Writing a time past sixty — Count on to the next hour, then add what is left. 9:45 plus 30 minutes is 10:15, never 9:75." Distinct texts rose from 48 to 162, repetition fell to 43.0%, and no lesson still shows the generic label. The residue is mostly legitimate: the same objective recurs across grades and across the Standard and Foundation tracks.

**Sharper "What should we look for?" questions.** The notice question fell through to a topic-wide default for many lessons, so 14 multiplication and division lessons all asked "What makes multiplication and division a useful pair?", from P1 equal groups to P4 long division. 22 objective-specific questions were added across multiplication and division, volume, whole numbers, addition and subtraction, graphs, fractions and percentage, covering 69 lessons. Distinct questions rose from 87 to 107.

**Consistency repairs.**
- 27 objective titles were missing the commas in their lists, and they appear as lesson titles throughout the app: "Use hundreds tens and ones" is now "Use hundreds, tens and ones", and "Recall the 6 7 8 and 9 multiplication tables" now reads "Recall the 6, 7, 8 and 9 multiplication tables". Five builder rules matched the old comma-free wording, so they were widened; a before-and-after snapshot of all 265 lessons confirmed no teaching content changed branch.
- Mixed-number questions gave improper-fraction answers: "Calculate 2 1/2 + 2 5/6" expected 16/3, against an objective called "Add and subtract mixed numbers". These now answer 5 1/3, and the improper form is still marked correct. 10 questions were affected; "Write 1 1/2 as an improper fraction" correctly still expects 3/2.
- Four-digit numbers appeared both as "6,908" and as "8084" in the same grade. A single rule in `gen.ts` now writes four digits plain and uses commas from five, matching the dominant usage and MOE practice.
- 13 visible strings used straight apostrophes where the app otherwise uses typographic ones (1157 of them), including "Let's practise" and "We aren't adding". One test asserted the old spelling and was updated.

Verification: 443 tests in 28 files pass, typecheck and production build succeed. A check confirms no arithmetic warm-up is now heavier than the lesson it opens. In the browser at phone width with no console errors and no horizontal overflow, the P6 circle and P3 durations lessons were opened and their new warm-ups answered and marked, and the new misconception was read in the adult guide.

Limits: this is a repetition and consistency pass, not new teaching material, and no qualified teacher has reviewed the new text. A generated warm-up tests the mapped prerequisite, which is occasionally a multi-step problem rather than a quick recall; the mapped objective, not a teacher, chose it. Explanation picture frames (53.1% repeated) and lesson hooks (47.2%) were measured but not addressed, and 55 notice questions are still shared by more than one objective. The Learn bundle is now 772 kB (238 kB gzip), still above the build's 500 kB warning.

# Question support rebuilt around real misconceptions — 18 September 2026

The generated lessons' help content was rebuilt. Previously one function attached the same kind of help to every question: wrong answers came from four arithmetic rules (so “50% of 120” listed 120.5), “I still don't get it” asked a definition question, “Show me another way” was the same generic method on 1,616 of 1,780 occasions, and reasoning items were two-option questions with fixed stems.

New modules in `src/teach/depth/catalogue/`:

- `facts.ts` recognises what each generated question asks (about ninety question types, from place value and rounding to percentage change, ratio shares, durations, triangle area and graph reading) by matching the families' sentence templates.
- `mistakes.ts` computes the wrong answers children actually give for that situation, each with a specific explanation: 52 − 27 = 35 (smaller digit from larger), 47 + 38 = 75 (dropped exchange), 5.4 + 3.47 = 8.51 (tenths read as hundredths), 570 × 3 = 1570 (only the 500 multiplied), 9:45 to 10:15 = 70 minutes (hour treated as 100 minutes), 21 in 1 : 2 : 4 shared as 7 (equal shares instead of units), a ruler read from 1, the other protractor scale. A plausibility filter rejects answers no child would give (non-integers from whole-number questions, negatives, absurd magnitudes).
- `strategies.ts` supplies genuinely different methods worked with the question's own numbers (count up for subtraction, round and adjust, 10% blocks or a fraction for percentages, the percentage you pay for discounts, a common denominator or decimals for fractions, unit value or a fraction of the total for ratio).
- `assess.ts` builds every generated question. “I still don't get it” is now the same question with no number larger and a smaller total, found by searching the same generator. Reasoning items are error analysis (“Ravi works out 499 × 9 and gets 3699. What went wrong?”) whose options are the diagnoses of different mistakes. Work-backwards and missing-number questions (□ + 2.55 = 12.41) appear where the mathematics supports them, and bar-model questions with a hidden unknown provide the unfamiliar representation, so mastery covers up to seven facets.

Other content fixes: multiplication and division steps split by place value without empty parts (570 × 3 was “570 + 0”); P1–P2 questions read “What is 5 + 4?” rather than “Calculate”; ordinal multiples read “2nd”, “3rd”; “in simplest form” is now enforced for percentage and decimal conversions; the P1 “Count and represent numbers to 100” focus now matches its tens-and-ones questions; the notice options are no longer always in the same order; and the recovery panel says “an easier one like it” when that is what it shows. The lesson revision is now `catalogue-depth-2026-09-18-v2`, so unfinished drafts of changed lessons restart cleanly while recorded achievements are kept.
